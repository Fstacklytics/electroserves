ElectroServes was founded in Dar es Salaam in 2014 by Amina Mwakalinga, after eight
years spent designing distribution systems for a regional contractor and watching too
many installations fail for reasons that were entirely predictable.

The company started with two technicians working out of a rented unit in Kurasini. It
now employs eighteen people and has completed more than 640 projects across the city.

## What we believe

**Diagnose before you quote.** Almost every problem we are called to has already been
"fixed" once by somebody who replaced a component without asking why it failed. We look
for the cause, and we tell you what we find even when it is not what you hoped to hear.

**Write it down.** Every circuit we touch is tested, and you receive the readings. Every
board we install is labelled. Every backup system we commission is handed over with a
diagram. This costs us time and saves clients a great deal of money later.

**Say no when no is the right answer.** We regularly talk clients out of larger systems
than they need, and we tell people when equipment is not worth repairing. We would
rather lose a sale than sell something that will disappoint.

## Our mission

To make electrical installations in Tanzania measurably safer, one properly tested
circuit at a time — and to leave every client better informed about their own
installation than they were before we arrived.

## Our vision

To be the contractor that other contractors call when a job is genuinely difficult.

## How we work

Every job follows the same shape regardless of size:

1. **Understand the problem.** A conversation, and for anything substantial, a site
   survey with measurements rather than estimates.
2. **Quote in writing.** A fixed price, a schedule, and a clear statement of what is
   excluded.
3. **Do the work.** Staged to minimise disruption, with clear communication about what
   is off and when.
4. **Test and document.** Full testing, written results, labelled boards, and a
   handover conversation.
5. **Stand behind it.** Twelve months on installation workmanship, and we answer the
   phone afterwards.

## Training

Six of our current technicians came through our apprenticeship programme, which takes
two intakes a year from local vocational colleges. Tanzania has a real shortage of
properly trained electricians, and we would rather be part of the solution than
complain about it.
