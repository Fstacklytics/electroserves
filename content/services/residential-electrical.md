Most of the homes we are called to were wired for a much smaller electrical load than
the family living there uses today. Add an air conditioner, a water heater and a
handful of extension leads, and a board that was adequate in 2005 starts tripping —
or worse, quietly overheating behind the wall.

## What we do on a residential job

We start with the distribution board, because that is where the evidence is. A visual
inspection plus an insulation resistance test tells us whether the problem is a single
faulty circuit or a system that has been extended one socket at a time without anyone
recalculating the load.

From there the work usually falls into one of three categories:

1. **Targeted repair** — a single failed circuit, a damaged cable run, or a socket
   that has burned out. Half a day, sometimes less.
2. **Board upgrade** — replacing an old fuse board with a modern consumer unit with
   RCD protection on every circuit. One full day for a typical three-bedroom house.
3. **Partial or full rewire** — where the existing cable insulation has degraded past
   the point of safe repair. We stage this room by room so you are never without power
   for a whole day.

## Safety first, always

Every circuit we touch is tested and certified before we leave, and you receive a
written record of the readings. If we find something dangerous that is outside the
scope of what you called us for, we tell you, we show you, and we quote separately —
we never quietly add it to the bill.
