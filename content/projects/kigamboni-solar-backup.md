The clinic's vaccine refrigerator had failed twice in one year during extended outages.
The existing generator started manually, which was of no help at all at 3am.

## Sizing the system

We logged the clinic's consumption for two weeks before quoting. The essential load —
two vaccine refrigerators, the treatment room, corridor lighting, the autoclave and the
records computer — came to 4.2kW peak and 31kWh per day.

The air conditioning in the administrative offices was deliberately excluded. Including
it would have almost doubled the cost of the system for a load that does not affect
patient safety.

## What was installed

A 10kVA hybrid inverter, 12kW of roof-mounted panels on a ballasted frame that does not
penetrate the waterproofing, and a 30kWh lithium battery bank.

Critically, we installed a **separate backup distribution board**. Staff can see at a
glance which circuits are protected, and nobody can accidentally move a load onto the
backup supply by plugging something into the wrong socket.

## Result

Since commissioning in February the clinic has ridden through every outage without
interruption to refrigeration. The generator remains as a third layer of redundancy but
has not been started in anger since.
