Electrical failures are rarely sudden. They announce themselves as heat for weeks or
months before anything actually stops working.

## The physics is simple

A good electrical connection has very low resistance. A loose or corroded one has more,
and resistance under load produces heat — proportional to the square of the current.

That heat degrades the surrounding insulation, which increases resistance further. The
process accelerates towards a failure that always looks sudden from the outside.

## What we look for

We scan the board under normal load with the covers off, comparing each phase against
the others. Three patterns matter:

- **A single hot terminal** while its neighbours are cool: a loose termination. This is
  the classic finding and the cheapest possible fix.
- **One hot phase across the whole board**: load imbalance. Not urgent, but it is
  wasting energy and shortening equipment life.
- **A hot breaker body** rather than its terminals: the device itself is failing
  internally and should be replaced.

## What counts as hot

We work to a delta rather than an absolute figure — the difference between a component
and comparable components under similar load. Broadly:

- 10–20°C above reference: monitor, re-scan at the next visit
- 20–40°C above reference: schedule a repair
- Over 40°C above reference: shut down and repair now

## Why twice a year

Terminations loosen through thermal cycling, and thermal cycling is seasonal. A scan
before and after the hottest part of the year catches the movement that has actually
happened rather than what a calendar says should have.

For most of our commercial clients, one avoided shutdown pays for a decade of scans.
