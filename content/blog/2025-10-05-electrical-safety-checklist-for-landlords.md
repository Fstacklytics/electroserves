A change of tenancy is the only reliable opportunity a landlord gets to inspect an
installation properly. Here is what we check, and what we tell owners to do about it.

## Between every tenancy

### The distribution board

Is there RCD or RCBO protection? If the board is rewireable fuses, that is the single
highest-value upgrade available and it should be planned rather than deferred again.

Is every circuit labelled? An unlabelled board makes every future fault slower and more
expensive to diagnose.

### Sockets and switches

Look for scorching, cracks, loose fixings and any socket that feels warm. Warmth at a
socket face means a loose termination behind it, and loose terminations are the origin
of most electrical fires.

### Outdoor and wet areas

Any socket outdoors or in a bathroom should be RCD protected without exception. Check
the seals on outdoor enclosures — they degrade quickly in coastal humidity.

### Appliances you supply

If you provide a water heater, cooker or air conditioner, it is part of your
installation for practical purposes. Have it inspected with everything else.

## Document it

Keep the test certificate, the circuit schedule and dated photographs of the board.
This matters twice: for your insurer, and for the conversation that follows if a tenant
ever reports a shock.

## Findings that should stop a letting

We advise owners not to hand over keys until these are resolved:

- Any exposed live conductor
- Absent earthing on socket circuits
- Scorching or heat damage at the board
- An RCD that does not trip when tested
- Any reported shock from a tap, pipe or appliance casing

Everything else can generally be scheduled. These five cannot.
