A breaker that trips is not a faulty breaker. It is a breaker doing exactly what it was
installed to do. The useful question is which of three quite different problems it is
responding to.

## Overload

The most common cause, and the least alarming. Too much current is being drawn through
one circuit — typically a kettle, an iron and a heater sharing a single socket circuit.

You can identify an overload by its timing: it trips when you switch on that last
appliance, and it resets happily once you unplug something.

The fix is either to redistribute appliances across circuits or, if it happens
regularly, to add a dedicated circuit for the high-load appliance.

## Short circuit

A live conductor touching a neutral. This trips instantly and forcefully, usually with
an audible bang, and often with a visible scorch mark or a smell.

Do not reset it. A short circuit that has already caused damage will cause more damage
on each reset attempt.

## Earth fault

Current is escaping to earth — often through a person. This is the one your RCD is
there for, and it is the reason a modern board matters.

Earth faults frequently trace back to water ingress: a leaking water heater element, a
flooded outdoor socket, or a washing machine that has been leaking quietly for weeks.

## The rule we give clients

Reset a breaker **once**. If it holds, note what you had switched on and think about
load distribution.

If it trips again immediately, or if there is any smell of burning, any scorching, or
any warmth around the board — stop. Switch off at the main isolator and call an
electrician.

Repeatedly resetting a breaker onto a genuine fault is how electrical fires start.
