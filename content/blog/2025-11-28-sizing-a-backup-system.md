Almost every backup system quote we are asked to review is sized on the wrong number.

## Power is not energy

An inverter is rated in kVA — how much it can deliver at any instant. A battery bank is
rated in kWh — how much total energy it stores.

You need enough kVA to start your largest load, and enough kWh to run everything for
however long the outage lasts. These are separate calculations, and quoting one while
ignoring the other is how people end up with a large inverter attached to a battery bank
that flattens in forty minutes.

## Start with what must stay on

Walk the property and write down only what genuinely cannot be off:

- Refrigeration
- Enough lighting to move around safely
- Router and phone charging
- Water pump, if you have no gravity supply
- Any medical or business-critical equipment

Then be honest about what does not make the list. Air conditioning and water heating are
the two loads that most inflate backup costs, and excluding them typically halves the
system price.

## Do the arithmetic

For each item: watts × hours per day = watt-hours. Add them up.

A typical Dar es Salaam household that has been honest about the list lands somewhere
between 4kWh and 8kWh per day of genuinely essential load. That is a much smaller and
cheaper system than most people are quoted.

## Then add the losses

Two adjustments before you buy:

1. **Depth of discharge.** Lithium can safely use around 80–90% of its rating; lead-acid
   closer to 50%. A 10kWh lead-acid bank gives you 5kWh of usable energy.
2. **Round-trip efficiency.** Expect to lose 10–15% in conversion.

So a 6kWh daily requirement means roughly 8kWh of usable storage, which means a 16kWh
lead-acid bank or a 10kWh lithium one.

## Why we log before quoting

We put a meter on the property for a week or two before quoting anything substantial.
Estimates are consistently wrong in both directions, and a system sized on a guess is
either a waste of money or a disappointment.
